Cognitive Load Theory

Working memory holds only about four chunks at once, so a task doesn't get gradually harder past that limit — extra items simply fall out.

A network router behaves the same way: once its buffer is full, packets aren't slowed, they're dropped without warning. That's why an overloaded mind misses the step it was sure it knew. Cut the clutter (extraneous load) and the idea itself gets room to fit.
