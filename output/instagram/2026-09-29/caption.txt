Signal Amplification: One Molecule Triggers a Thousand-Fold Response

A single hormone molecule binds a single receptor on a cell's surface — but that one binding event activates dozens of enzyme molecules inside the cell, and each of those activates dozens more downstream, doubling and redoubling at every step.

By the time the cascade reaches its final product, one molecule of signal outside the cell has produced thousands of molecules of response inside it. The cell doesn't need a loud signal; it needs a good amplifier — which is why hormone concentrations in blood can be almost undetectably small and still trigger a fast, full-body response.
