Protein Folding: Chaperones Bypass the Misfolding Trap

A protein doesn't just fold into any stable shape — it has to reach one specific, functional structure, and the energy landscape guiding it has side valleys where it can get stuck in a stable but useless misfolded state.

Chaperone proteins don't force the correct shape directly; they just block those traps, buying the molecule time to find its way to the true minimum. It's the same problem a startup procedure solves in a plant: a unit can settle into a stable-but-wrong operating point if it isn't guided through the right sequence, so start-up interlocks exist to block the traps along the way, not to force the target state directly.
