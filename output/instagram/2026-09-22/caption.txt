Social Proof: When the Crowd Becomes the Evidence

When you're not sure what's true or what to do, other people's behavior carries real information — if a hundred people just walked out of a building, that's evidence something's wrong, even before you smell smoke. That's rational, not just herd instinct.

But it creates a threshold effect: your own uncertainty barely moves while only a few others have acted, then collapses fast once enough have. That's why adoption of anything uncertain — a new tool, a rumor, an evacuation — tends to look flat for a long stretch, then suddenly tip, rather than climb steadily from day one.
