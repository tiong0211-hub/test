Game Theory and the Nash Equilibrium

A Nash equilibrium is a set of choices where no player can do better by changing their own strategy alone — which makes it stable, but not necessarily good.

Think of a ball resting in a valley in physics: every single nudge rolls it back, so it's a stable state — even when a deeper valley exists that only a coordinated push could reach. In the prisoner's dilemma, mutual defection is exactly that shallow valley: neither side can improve by moving alone, yet both would be better off cooperating.
